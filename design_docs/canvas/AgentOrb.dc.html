Anatomy & rules
Why a network. This is what the agent is, structurally: many simple nodes, connections between them, and activity that is a pattern of signals moving along those connections. Nothing is hidden behind a surface; it is seen through, not looked at.
Nucleus. A sparse shell of nodes on a sphere and a denser core cluster for what is currently held. Each node links to its nearest neighbours; core nodes also reach the shell. The whole graph turns very slowly so depth reads.
Rings. Two to four concentric broken rings face the viewer around the nucleus: arcs of uneven length and brightness with gaps between, a fine tick ring among them, alternating direction and period. They are the instrument around the mind: they speed up with reading and running, stop and breathe bright when the agent needs you, and slow when raw access is on.
Signal, not spin. The primary motion is a pulse travelling along an edge. Pathways are tiered; each state enables tiers and sets speed, so idle fires a few, reading many, running all. No state performs "thinking" while nothing is happening.
Direction means something. Reading converges inward. Wrote lights a path that leaves the sphere: an output actually exited. Needs-you holds one pathway bright and still while the rings stop and breathe — blocked on a person, from the inside.
Colour. One token per state (--agent-idle … --agent-raw); nodes, edges, pulses and halo are mixed from it. No face, no eyes, no orientation toward the viewer — ever.
Later. The shared-focus handoff becomes a bright entry point on the shell where the human's context enters. Under prefers-reduced-motion: freeze the turn, keep pulses slow, keep colour and the ring.
